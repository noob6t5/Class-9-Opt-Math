DATA

Sometimes numbers are close.
Sometimes they are far apart.

Today we learn how to measure how spread out the numbers are.

First: What is Dispersion?

Dispersion simply means:

“How much are the numbers spread out?”

So, dispersion tells us about the spread of the data.

What is Mean Deviation?

Imagine every number asking:

“How far am I from the center?”

Mean Deviation finds the average distance of all the numbers from a center.

Mean Deviation from Mean

Mean → Center

We take the distance of every number from the Mean.

Mean Deviation from Median

Median → Center

Same idea. But now we measure every number from the Median.

Why do we have Mean and Median?

Mean

Uses all values to find the center.

Median

Uses the middle value after arranging the data.

Very large or very small values can pull the Mean away. The Median is less affected.

Now imagine this...

One number is very far away.

The Mean moves toward it. The Median usually does not move much.

So what is Quartile Deviation?

Quartile Deviation does not look at every number.

It looks at the middle 50% of the data.

It uses two points: Q₁ and Q₃.

Think of the data like a group of students

The yellow box represents the middle 50%.

Q₁ is the left side. Q₃ is the right side.

Quartile Deviation asks:

“How wide is the middle part?”
QD = (Q₃ − Q₁) ÷ 2

So QD tells us how spread out the middle half of the data is.

Mean Deviation vs Quartile Deviation

Mean Deviation

Looks at all numbers.

Measures their average distance from Mean or Median.

Quartile Deviation

Looks at the middle 50%.

Less affected by very large or very small values.

What does Coefficient mean?

Coefficient simply means we want to express something as a relative value.

Instead of only asking:

“How much spread?”

we can ask:

“How much spread compared with the data?”

Coefficient of Quartile Deviation

CQD = (Q₃ − Q₁) ÷ (Q₃ + Q₁)

It is based on the same Q₁ and Q₃.

But now we get a relative measure, which helps us compare different datasets.

One simple way to remember

MD

“How far are the numbers from the center?”

QD

“How wide is the middle 50%?”

Coefficient

“How large is the spread compared with the data?”

THAT'S IT.

Three ideas. One topic: SPREAD.

Mean Deviation → all values.

Quartile Deviation → middle 50%.

Coefficient → relative comparison.
